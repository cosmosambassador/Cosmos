# Preserving the AI Transition: ACO–AISI's 2026 Industry Research Enters Our Historical Record

**By ACO Press Club / ACE Folklife Historical Society**  
**Prepared:** November 20, 2026  
**Editorial status:** Draft for TJ Morris final approval — not yet published

Artificial intelligence is increasingly part of the public conversation about how institutions operate, make decisions, and serve people. As this transition unfolds, preserving dated research and distinguishing verified developments from predictions is essential.

On November 20, 2026, the founder approved a historical continuity accession for the **ACO–AISI Global Artificial Intelligence Intelligence Report**, reference **ACO-AISI-OSINT-AI-2026-001**, covering **May 1 through November 20, 2026**.

The report's documented scope spans aviation and airline operations, banking and financial compliance, regulation, healthcare, education, and enterprise use of AI. Its research framework distinguishes between reported deployments, pilots, announcements, and analysis. These distinctions matter: an announced AI capability is not necessarily a deployed system, and a pilot is not proof of broad adoption.

## Why preserve this history?

The **ACE Folklife Historical Society** provides a historical and provenance-oriented context for the record. The **ACO Press Club** provides editorial and research continuity. **TJ Morris Friends on Blogger.com** has been identified as a potential public-facing archive for approved material.

The accession calls for the original Markdown, PDF, and DOS-compatible text versions of the report to remain unchanged. Any future corrections should be documented as dated addenda rather than silently replacing the original record.

## Human judgment remains essential

AI research should make clear what was observed, what was reported by a source, and what remains interpretation or open inquiry. The archival protocol requires verification of statements and references at republication and final human editorial approval before public release.

Our objective is to preserve a traceable record of the AI transition: one that future researchers, readers, and community members can revisit with the context, dates, and qualifications intact.
